1. Why U-Net instead of YOLO?
The task is pixel-level segmentation with RLE masks. U-Net directly predicts
aligned masks and combines context with spatial detail. YOLO detection would
produce boxes; YOLO segmentation variants exist, but were not benchmarked here.

2. Why a lightweight U-Net?
482,788 parameters and widths16/32/64/128 fit the6GB GPU and permit controlled
pipeline comparisons with fixed architecture. Small size does not prove accuracy.

3. How does convolution work?
A shared learned kernel slides over local pixels, multiplies and sums across
positions/channels. Our blocks use3x3,stride1,padding1, so spatial size stays.
Open phase2/model.py:ConvBlock; first conv has3*16*3*3=432 weights.

4. Why ReLU?
max(0,z) adds nonlinearity cheaply. Its positive-side derivative is1 and
negative-side derivative0. Our final logits are not passed through ReLU.

5. Why max pooling?
2x2 pooling with stride2 halves dimensions and increases contextual coverage,
reducing compute while losing location detail. Encoder skips retain finer maps.

6. What do skip connections do?
torch.cat joins encoder features with same-resolution upsampled decoder maps.
For dec3,64+64 becomes128 channels. It restores detail and aids gradient flow.

7. What is the bottleneck?
The deepest ConvBlock,128 channels at32x32 for a256-square patch. It combines
compressed context before the decoder. It has no attention/pretrained component.

8. How does output retain spatial dimensions?
Padding preserves sizes within blocks; three pools halve, three stride2
transposed convolutions double. The1x1 output convolution keeps256x256, emits4.
Full256x1600 is recovered by probability stitching, not stretching one patch.

9. What are backpropagation and the chain rule?
Backprop computes loss derivatives through composed operations. The chain rule
multiplies local derivatives and adds branch contributions. Example z=w*x,
unweighted BCE gives dL/dw=(sigmoid(z)-y)*x before averaging. Adam applies them.

10. How does Adam update weights?
It keeps exponential gradient/gradient-square moments, corrects initial bias,
and subtracts lr*mhat/(sqrt(vhat)+eps). Actual lr0.001,betas0.9/0.999,eps1e-8.
K restores J's Adam state; it does not reset optimizer history.

11. Why combine BCE and Dice?
BCE supplies pixel-level supervision, including background; soft Dice emphasizes
foreground overlap. Our baseline mixes them equally. Imbalance can still cause
collapse; different Dice reductions and sampling were explicit experiments.

12. What EXACT objective did final K use?
0.5 unweighted BCE +0.5 inverse-training-positive-pair-frequency weighted Dice.
GT-empty Dice terms are zero; the mean denominator is all batch*4 pairs.
Smoothing1; weights are from training exposure only. It is not E's weighted BCE.
Show phase2/objectives.py:balanced_present_dice_loss and K config.

13. Why sigmoid rather than softmax?
Four independent binary labels can overlap. Sigmoid converts each channel's
logit independently. Softmax imposes one normalized category distribution.
Background is implicit when all four predictions are below their thresholds.

14. What is Dice?
2TP/(2TP+FP+FN), computed per class after summing split-wide pixel counts.
For TP6,FP2,FN4,Dice=2/3. Evaluation Dice is unsmoothed binary foreground F1.

15. What is IoU?
TP/(TP+FP+FN), overlap divided by union. The same example gives0.5.
K macro IoU0.137937 is averaged per class; don't convert macro Dice nonlinearly.

16. Why macro averaging?
Each defect class contributes equally, exposing rare-class failure that a
pixel-weighted aggregate can hide. All normal images still contribute FP counts.
metrics.py uses int64 counts; undefined ratios are null, not free perfect scores.

17. What is class imbalance, and why not pixel accuracy?
Background and class3 dominate while class2 is rare. An all-background mask can
have high pixel accuracy and zero defect recall. Use Dice/IoU,precision/recall,
class-specific errors and normal-image alarms; crop/weighting interventions help
exposure but do not automatically improve every metric.

18. How were image-mask pairs and RLE prepared?
Four RLE strings decode to native4x256x1600 masks before aligned image/mask
cropping. RLE is one-based column-major: for height3,'3 2' crosses from row2
of column0 to row0 of column1. dataset_utils.decode_rle uses reshape(order='F').
Whole-image masks use nearest-neighbor resize; native patch masks are not resized.

19. How did you avoid train/test leakage?
Seed42 splits original-training exact-RGB duplicate groups; validation stays
unchanged. New candidates use9048 train,2514 val,1006 test; training-only priors,
val-only epoch/threshold selection and pre-test lock. Phase I saw future test
images, so old weights are excluded as an independent test baseline. Near/coil
duplicates remain unverified; the test is now opened and cannot tune new choices.

20. Why overlapping patches?
Keep native detail with bounded memory and multiple predictions near borders.
Stride128 yields12 patches; final x1344 covers width1600. Training selects one
crop/source/epoch; inference evaluates every patch without ground truth.

21. How does weighted stitching work?
Multiply each sigmoid map by outer(max(hann,0.01),max(hann,0.01)), accumulate
at native coordinates, divide by summed positive weights, then threshold.
Patch centers count more. Code: patches.weight_window and predict.

22. What did ablations prove?
Matched C patch-only Dice0.0918<B whole0.1239; F patch<G whole too. Patching
alone failed at these budgets. Fixed C Hann/overlap variants test reconstruction;
F/H/J isolate loss reduction/sampling/weighting, and K extends J's budget.
K0.2340 exceeds B but changes objective,crops and9-vs3 epochs. Single-seed
contrasts support limited findings, not a universal causal or literature claim.

23. Why did the baseline underperform?
Measured: B predicts mainly class3; rare-class recall is zero. Imbalance, short
training and resized fine detail are plausible causes, not independently proven
explanations. C also underperformed B, so resolution alone is not the answer.

24. What are the final model's limitations and generalization evidence?
K macro Dice0.233996/IoU0.137937 is modest. Class2 Dice0.082752 on20 positive
test images;452/472 GT-empty images alarm at any pixel. One seed, limited
budget, no external-domain/coil-group validation. It is a research prototype.

25. What happens on a defect-free image?
Ideally all masks are empty; this model often produces false alarms. Background
is implicit, not a fifth output. Show the actual predictions; no target means
unknown accuracy. A blank mask can also be a missed defect, not proof of clean steel.

26. How would you reduce false alarms?
Evaluate thresholds/calibration, component-size filtering or better negative
training on validation, measuring recall tradeoffs. These controls are proposed,
not enabled/validated fixes. Reserve a new untouched test for another final claim.
Live threshold edits are exploratory and leave the locked model unchanged.
